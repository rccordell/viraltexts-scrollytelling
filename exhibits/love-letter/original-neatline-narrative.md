# Original Neatline exhibit narrative (recovered from the Wayback Machine)

Recovered 2026-09-20 from `https://web.archive.org/web/20191125153050/http://loveletter.viraltexts.org/`
(and other nearby captures of the same page). The original Neatline exhibit embedded its
full authored narrative directly in the page as an HTML field (`Neatline.g.neatline.exhibit.narrative`),
so this is the **exact original wording**, including `<sic>` tags the curators used to mark
OCR/transcription quirks in the source letter as deliberate scholarly apparatus (not typos to fix).

`data-neatline-slug="..."` marks exactly which word or phrase was tied to a Neatline
annotation record. **The annotation record's own title/body text — the actual commentary
explaining each connection — was not recoverable** (see note at the bottom). Only the slugs
and which text they're attached to survived.

## Full narrative HTML

```html
<h1>A "Stunning" Love Letter to <span data-neatline-slug="viral-texts">Viral Texts</span></h1>

<p>Like most nineteenth-century newspapers, <span data-neatline-slug="raftsmans-journal"><em>The Raftsman's Journal</em></span> sought to connect its readers in rural <span data-neatline-slug="clearfield">Clearfield, Pennsylvania</span> with wider worlds of news, information, and literature. Whether published in major metropolitan areas such as New York, Boston, and Philadelphia; in smaller cities such as Wheeling or Nashville; or in rural towns such as Clearfield, nineteenth-century newspapers relied on networks of exchange for much of their content. Newspaper editors subscribed to each others' newspapers, which came to them in the mail on post roads or, later, railroads.</p>

<p>When exchange papers arrived, <span data-neatline-slug="country-editor">editors would comb through them to find content their readers would appreciate, which they would then clip out with scissors</span> and paste on sheets for their compositors to set in new type for <span data-neatline-slug="nov-4">the next daily, weekly, or irregular edition</span>, sometimes changing the original text in the process. Sometimes a clipping would not be needed immediately, but would be saved for later use; we find clusters of reprinted texts that circulated in this way around the country over years or even decades.</p>

<p>Thus texts of all kinds—including <span data-neatline-slug="murders">news</span>, <span data-neatline-slug="dashford-tragedy">fiction</span>, <span data-neatline-slug="marjories-almanac">poetry</span>, <span data-neatline-slug="going-rounds">vignettes</span>, how-to columns, <span data-neatline-slug="men-wanted">lists</span>, <span data-neatline-slug="roger-williams">descriptions of scientific and historical curiosities</span>, <span data-neatline-slug="manners">etiquette</span>, <span data-neatline-slug="keep-warm">medical and health notes</span>, <span data-neatline-slug="good-rule">business advice</span>, <span data-neatline-slug="home-cheerfulness">parenting advice</span>, recipes, <span data-neatline-slug="enjoy-blessings">religious affirmations</span>, <span data-neatline-slug="eight-hour">jokes</span>, and more—circulated around the country, connecting readers from New England to New Orleans to California through shared texts.</p>

<p>This exhibit is intended to hint at the breadth—and the oddities—of nineteenth-century reprinting that we have found thus far in <span data-neatline-slug="viral-texts">the Viral Texts Project</span>. If you <span data-neatline-slug="page">peruse the page</span>, you will find articles that link to our database, where you can browse versions that appeared in other newspapers, or related pieces.</p>

<p align="center"><big><big>———— § § § ————</big></big></p>

<p>We focus, however, on the <span data-neatline-slug="loveletter">"sublimely 'splendiferous'" love letter</span> reprinted in the middle of the page. This is one of our favorite "viral texts": it's funny and over the top—no doubt both reasons for its popularity—but in its humor it speaks also to many common themes across the widely-reprinted texts we have found. To that end, we have carefully annotated this edition of "A 'stunning' Love Letter" to point out to related texts in our database. We are not claiming that the writer of "Love Letter" had these other texts in mind. Instead, we hope this provides a fun way to begin exploring the vast range of topics, genres, styles, and tones that comprised the nineteenth-century newspaper.</p>

<blockquote>
<p><span data-neatline-slug="loveletter">A "stunning" Love Letter.</span></p>

<p><span data-neatline-slug="splendiferous">The following is sublimely 'splendiferous,'</span> and we <sic>cecommend</sic> it as a model to letter writers:</p>

<p><span data-neatline-slug="miss-c">MY DEAR MISS C.</span>—Every time I think of you my heart flops <span data-neatline-slug="churn-dasher">up and down like a churn dasher</span>. Sensations of unutterable joy creep over it <span data-neatline-slug="young-goats">like young goats</span> over a stable roof, and thrill through it like <span data-neatline-slug="tow-linen">Spanish needles through a pair of tow linen trowsers</span>. As a gosling swimeth with delight in a mud-puddle, so swim I <span data-neatline-slug="sea-of-glory">in a sea of glory</span>. Visions of ecstatic rapture, thicker than the <span data-neatline-slug="boot-blacking">hairs in a blacking brush</span>, and brighter than <span data-neatline-slug="humming-bird">the hue of the humming bird's pinions</span>, <sic>visi</sic> me in my slumbers, and borne on their visible wings, your image stands before me, and I reach out to grasp it, like a pointer snapping at <span data-neatline-slug="blue-bottle-fly">a blue bottle fly</span>. When I first beheld your angelic perfections, I was bewildered, and my brain whirled about like a bumble bee <span data-neatline-slug="glass-tumbler">under a glass tumbler</span>. My eyes stood open like cellar doors in a country town, and I lifted up my ears to catch the silvery accents of your voice. My <span data-neatline-slug="tongue-wag">tongue refused to wag</span>, and in silent adoration I drank in the sweet infection of love <span data-neatline-slug="whiskey-punch">as a thirsty man swalloweth a hot whiskey punch</span>. Since the light of your face fell upon my life, I sometimes feel as if I could lift myself up by my bootstraps to <span data-neatline-slug="presbyterian-steeple">the top of the presbyterian steeple</span>, and pull the bell rope for singing school. Day and night you are in my thoughts. <span data-neatline-slug="blushing-bride">When Aurora, blushing like a bride, rises from her saffron couch</span>; and when the jay bird pipes his tuneful lay in the apple tree, by the spring house; <span data-neatline-slug="chanticleer">when the chanticleer's shrill clarion heralds the coming morn</span>; when the awakened pig ariseth from his bed and grunteth, and goeth for his morning refreshments; when <span data-neatline-slug="drowsy-beetle">the drowsy beetle wheels his drowning flight</span>, at sultry noontide, and <span data-neatline-slug="milking-time">when the lowing cows come home at milking time</span>, I think of thee; and like a piece of gum elastic, my heart seems to stretch clean across my bosom. <span data-neatline-slug="sorrel-horse">Your hair is like the mane of a sorrel horse, powdered with gold</span>; and the brass pin skewered through your waterfall, fills me with unbounded awe. <span data-neatline-slug="your-forehead">Your forehead is <sic>smoothar</sic> than the elbow of an old coat</span>. Your eyes are glorious to behold. In their liquid depths I see <span data-neatline-slug="cupids">legions of little Cupids</span>, bathing like a cohort of ants in an old army cracker. When their fire hit upon <span data-neatline-slug="anatomy">my manly breast, it penetrated my entire anatomy</span> like a load of bird-shot would go <sic>thro</sic> a rotten apple. <span data-neatline-slug="parian-marble">Your nose is from a chunk of Parian marble</span>, and your mouth puckered with sweetness. Nectar lingers on your lips like <span data-neatline-slug="bears-paw">honey on a bear's paw</span>, and myriads of unfledged kisses are there ready to fly out an light somewhere, like blue birds out of the parent's nest. <span data-neatline-slug="wind-harp">Your laugh rings on my ears like the wind-harp's strains</span>, or the bleat of a stray lamb on the bleak hillside. The dimples in your cheek are like bowers in <span data-neatline-slug="bed-roses">beds of roses</span>, or hollows in a cake of <span data-neatline-slug="home-made-sugar">home-made sugar</span>.</p>

<p>I am dying to fly to your presence and pour out <span data-neatline-slug="burning-eloquence">the burning eloquence of my love</span>, <span data-neatline-slug="thrifty-housewife">as a thrifty house wife pours out coffee</span>. Away from you I am as melancholy as a sick rat. <span data-neatline-slug="june-bugs">Sometimes I can hear the June bugs of despondency buzzing in my ears</span>, and feel <span data-neatline-slug="cold-lizards">the cold lizards of despair crawling down my back</span>. Uncouth feats, like a thousand minnows, nibble at my spirits, and my soul is pierced through with doubts, as an old cheese with skippers.</p>

<p>My love for you is stronger than the smell of <span data-neatline-slug="patent-butter">Coffy's patent butter</span>, or the kick of a young cow, and more unselfish than a kitten's first caterwaul. As the song bird hankers for the light of day, the cautious mouse for <span data-neatline-slug="fresh-bacon">the fresh bacon in the trap</span>, as a lean pup hankers after new milk, so I long for thee.</p>

<p><span data-neatline-slug="speckled-pullet">You are fairer than a speckled pullet</span>, sweeter than a yankee doughnut fried in <span data-neatline-slug="sorgum-molasses"><sic>sorgum</sic> molasses</span>, brighter than the topknot plumage in the head of a muscovy duck. <span data-neatline-slug="patent-butter">You are candy kisses, raisins, and pound cake and sweetened toddy altogether</span>.</p>

<p>If these few remarks will enable you to see the inside of my soul, and me to win your affections, I shall be as happy as a woodpecker <span data-neatline-slug="cherry-tree">on a cherry tree</span>, or a stage horse in a green pasture. If you cannot reciprocate my thrilling passion, <span data-neatline-slug="poisoned-bedbug">I will pine away like a poisoned bedbug</span>, and falling away from the flourishing vine of life, and untimely branch; and in the coming years, when the shadows grow from the hills, and <span data-neatline-slug="june-bugs">the <sic>philisophic</sic> frog sings his cheerful evening hymns</span>, you, happy in another's love, can come and drop a tear and catch a cold <span data-neatline-slug="last-resting-place">upon the last resting place of,</span></p>

<p align="right"><span data-neatline-slug="last-resting-place">JULIUS EPAMINONDAS MUGGINS.</span></p>
</blockquote>
```

## What this confirms/adds versus our current rebuild

- **A missing transition paragraph** ("We focus, however, on the...") between the general
  intro and the letter itself — not currently in `exhibit.json`'s `intro`.
- **A fuller, more specific credits line** naming Ryan Cordell, Jonathan Fitzgerald, and Abby
  Mullen by name, plus Wayne Graham (UVA Scholars' Lab) and David McClure (Stanford CIDR),
  with their original links.
- **The intro's own genre list was itself a set of inline links** to the "Elsewhere on the
  page" articles — e.g. "news" → `murders`, "fiction" → `dashford-tragedy`, "poetry" →
  `marjories-almanac`, "vignettes" → `going-rounds`, "lists" → `men-wanted`, "descriptions of
  scientific and historical curiosities" → `roger-williams`, "etiquette" → `manners`,
  "medical and health notes" → `keep-warm`, "business advice" → `good-rule`, "parenting
  advice" → `home-cheerfulness`, "religious affirmations" → `enjoy-blessings`, "jokes" →
  `eight-hour`. Our rebuild currently only has 3 of these ("Marjorie's Almanac", "Who Ate
  Roger Williams?", "Hoofland's German Bitters") — the original apparently annotated at least
  9 more distinct articles on the page.
- **62 total `data-neatline-slug` annotations** (vs. our 42 + 3 = 45) — the original also
  linked phrases we didn't capture: `tow-linen`, `sea-of-glory`, `boot-blacking`,
  `humming-bird`, `blue-bottle-fly` (we have a similarly-named one), `glass-tumbler`,
  `tongue-wag`, `blushing-bride`, `chanticleer`, `milking-time`, `your-forehead`, `anatomy`,
  `parian-marble`, `bed-roses`, `home-made-sugar`, `burning-eloquence`, `thrifty-housewife`,
  `patent-butter` (used twice), `fresh-bacon`, `sorgum-molasses`, `cherry-tree`, plus the
  framing slugs `viral-texts`, `raftsmans-journal`, `clearfield`, `country-editor`, `nov-4`,
  `page`, `loveletter`, `splendiferous`, `miss-c`.
- The `<sic>` tags mark deliberate preservation of the original text's own OCR/typesetting
  quirks (`cecommend`, `visi`, `smoothar`, `thro`, `sorgum`, `philisophic`) as scholarly
  apparatus, not errors to silently correct.

## What was NOT recoverable

Each `data-neatline-slug` refers to a separate Neatline "record" — a database row with its
own title, a body of **commentary text** (the actual analysis explaining why that phrase
connects to something in the Viral Texts database), and a rectangular region on the newspaper
image. That commentary text is what carried the real analytical content, and it was **not**
recoverable:

- It was loaded dynamically via AJAX (`GET /neatline/records?exhibit_id=1&extent=...`), not
  embedded in the page's initial HTML — so a plain HTML crawl never captured it.
- The Wayback Machine's CDX index has only 3 captures of that endpoint, none with a
  populated `extent` parameter, and all return `{"records":[],"numFound":0}`.
- The live site (`loveletter.viraltexts.org`) still resolves, but its main routes 500 and the
  records API returns empty even with a deliberately huge bounding box — the underlying
  Omeka/Neatline application is broken, not just its front-end JS.
- The Omeka "items" some records may have referenced (`/items/show/35`–`44`, also archived)
  turned out to hold only bare bibliographic metadata (title/publisher/date) for the
  cross-referenced comparison texts, not the annotation commentary itself.
